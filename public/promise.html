Our promise to parents

How we keep AWTY safe for your children

AWTY is made for children aged 4 to 12, strapped into the back of a car. A short list of rules comes first in everything we build. If a new idea would break one, we don't build it. This page shows exactly what we do, step by step.

FreeNever uses AI. Emma's 200 fun facts are written and checked by us, and live on the phone.
£2.99 a monthAI writes fun facts about the places you pass. Every one is checked before your child hears it.
£5.99 a monthAI also writes stories for your trip and answers questions. Every word is checked.
Six promises

What we promise, and how we keep it

Names stay on the phone

Your children's names are never sent to our server or to any AI.

How: the app sends a stand-in word instead of their names. The phone puts the real names back just before the story is read.

The microphone stays off

AWTY never listens unless you decide it should.

How: it's off when the app is installed, and only a parent can switch it on, in the privacy choices.

Grown-up bits need your PIN

Children can't wander into settings, links or sharing.

How: unlocking the trip screen, links that leave the app, sharing and privacy choices all ask for your PIN. "Forgot PIN?" has to be held for 10 seconds, which keeps little fingers out.

Every AI word is checked

No story, fact or answer reaches your child without passing our safety checks.

How: eight checkpoints, from the moment it's asked for to the moment Emma reads it. They're all shown below.

Names stay private out loud too

In Free and £2.99, Emma's voice is made on the phone itself.

How: no voice we use, now or later, is ever sent your children's names. A story read by any other voice leaves the names out, or Emma says them.

You choose what's shared

A clear privacy screen asks you first, and asks again whenever anything changes.

How: we only learn how the app is used if you switch on "Help improve AWTY". Even then we never keep where you drove: place names are replaced with [place].

The journey of a fun fact or story

Eight checkpoints between the AI and your child

This is what happens every time AWTY asks the AI for a fun fact about a place you pass, or, in the £5.99 version, a story for your trip.

Where it happens:On your phoneOn our serverYou
  1. 1

    Nothing personal goes out

    On your phone

    For a fun fact, only the place and your child's age group are sent: never names, interests or where you're going. For a story, your children's names are replaced with a stand-in before anything leaves the phone, and our server turns away anything else.

    "…and Ava and Leo looked out of the window"becomes"…and {NAMES} looked out of the window"
  2. 2

    What you type is checked

    On our server

    For stories, if you add your child's interests, like "dinosaurs" or "trains", they're checked against our blocked-word list. Anything on it is left out of the request.

  3. 3

    The AI is given our rulebook

    On our server

    The AI writer gets the rules for your child's age group, and the full list of things it must never write. You can read both further down this page.

  4. 4

    Every sentence is read for problems

    On our server

    Our checker looks for blocked words, anything unsafe to copy in a car, and any time the story asks your child to move about in their seat. It also checks the sentences aren't too long or hard for their age.

  5. 5

    A second AI reviews it

    On our server

    A separate AI, whose only job is checking, reads every word against the same rulebook. It never writes anything itself.

  6. 6

    Fixed, or replaced with ours

    On our server

    If any check fails, it's written again with the problems pointed out, then checked again from the start. If it fails a second time, the app uses a fact or story we wrote and checked ourselves.

  7. 7

    Checked again on the phone

    On your phone

    The phone runs its own word check, and throws away anything that trips it. Only then are the names put back, and Emma reads it.

  8. 8

    You can report a problem

    You

    If anything doesn't feel right, tell us in Settings, under "Report a problem". We look at every report. When one shows a real problem, or our check results show a pattern, we add a new rule.

Fun facts

Only facts that are true and cheerful. Nothing about wars, battles, deaths, disasters, crimes or punishments.

Places on the way

Pubs, cemeteries, prisons, hospitals, casinos and battlefields never become stops in a story or a fact.

Answers to questions

Coming in the £5.99 version. Every answer is checked on our server, and if one fails, the phone gives a safe reply of its own.

The rulebook

What the AI is told, word for word in plain English

Never in a story or fact

  • Injury, illness or death
  • Monsters or ghosts
  • Anyone getting lost or scared
  • Strangers
  • Weapons or fighting
  • Romance
  • Brand names
  • Food or allergy dangers
  • Toilet humour
  • Anyone being mean or left out

Nor anything unsafe a child might copy in a car: undoing a seatbelt, distracting the driver, opening windows, throwing things, climbing, wandering off, talking to strangers, going near water or roads alone, or eating things they don't know.

All a story may ask your child to do

  • Look out of the window
  • Listen
  • Say something in a normal voice
  • Count
  • Sing quietly
  • Imagine

Nothing with their bodies, no loud noises that could startle the driver, and never waving at anyone outside the car. Every story also has to show kindness, teamwork and good manners.

Made for their age

Three age groups, three sets of rules

4 to 5
Sentences
Very short, about 8 to 10 everyday words
Stories
Tiny, cosy problems, like a cake that keeps wobbling or a kite stuck in a tree
Characters
Only kind ones. No baddies at all.
6 to 7
Sentences
Short, about 10 to 14 words, with a few new words explained by the story
Stories
Small adventures and gentle surprises
Characters
Can be cheeky, but never mean
8 to 12
Sentences
Longer, with richer words
Stories
Bigger quests and puzzles, but never real danger
Characters
A rival only if they become friends by the end
